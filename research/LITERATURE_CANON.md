# Machine Pidgin literature canon

**Status:** Living research index, first established 3 August 2026

**Last source check:** 19 August 2026

**Scope:** Human-to-machine communication standards and the evidence needed to
evaluate SPEAR without treating popularity, formalism, or citation count as proof.

Inclusion in this canon means that a source is useful for designing or falsifying the
Machine Pidgin research program. It does **not** mean that the Institute endorses every
claim in it. `Source fact` identifies what the source itself contributes. `SPEAR relevance`
is a Researcher inference, not a source claim. Normative standards, empirical evidence,
conceptual foundations, and provisional adjacent work are separated deliberately.

## Normative standards and stable technical specifications

| Source and provenance | Source fact | SPEAR relevance (Researcher inference) | Important limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| [ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html), published Nov. 2018, confirmed 2024; ISO now marks it “to be revised” | Defines requirements-engineering processes and required information-item content. | Baseline vocabulary for completeness, traceability, verification, and requirement quality. | Full text is paywalled; broad lifecycle standard, not a human-AI command language; revision is pending. |
| [ISO 9241-210:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html), confirmed May 2025 | Gives requirements and recommendations for human-centred design across interactive-system lifecycles. | SPEAR authoring burden, error recovery, and accessibility need contextual user evaluation, not syntax-only evaluation. | Process guidance; full text is paywalled; no task or authority semantics. |
| [ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html), released 15 Jan. 2025 by the ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group | Defines a controlled natural language for technical documentation through 53 writing rules and a controlled dictionary; the official current issue is available through the [maintainer's request page](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html). | A mature controlled-language baseline for testing whether constrained vocabulary and syntax reduce ambiguity and author/reader burden without erasing necessary qualifications. | Designed for technical documentation, not general dialogue or agent authorization. Clearer or shorter text does not prove preserved intent, factual correctness, completeness, accessibility for every reader, or model compliance; the standard is copyrighted and should not be republished without permission. |
| [BCP 14](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14): [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119.html) (1997) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174.html) (2017) | Defines conventional strength for uppercase MUST, SHOULD, MAY, and related terms. | A useful convention for constraint strength and conformance prose. | Lexical strength does not determine who has legitimate authority or how conflicts resolve. |
| [RFC 8259 — JSON](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8259.html), IETF Internet Standard, Dec. 2017 | Defines an interoperable text syntax for structured values. | A portable serialization substrate for SPEAR records and reproducible fixtures. | Syntax does not validate schemas, meaning, truth, authorization, or duplicate-key handling in every parser. |
| Unicode Standard Annex #14, [Unicode Line Breaking Algorithm, revision 55](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr14/tr14-55.html), stable normative annex for Unicode 17.0, Sept. 2025 | Defines line-breaking classes and conformance rules, including mandatory breaks for hard line-boundary functions and keeping CRLF together. | A stable basis for distinguishing text values from prompt structure and for specifying portable boundary handling. | It governs line-break opportunities, not authorization, sanitization, display behavior, tokenization, or which characters a SPEAR implementation should accept. |
| [JSON Schema Draft 2020-12](https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12), metaschemas published June 2022 | Defines machine-checkable vocabularies for JSON structure and validation. | Supports explicit field contracts and round-trip validation. | A passing schema establishes form, not preserved human intent or factual correctness. |
| [W3C SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/), Recommendation July 2017 | Defines shapes and constraints for validating RDF graphs. | A precedent for declaring semantic constraints separately from instances. | RDF authoring/tooling burden; graph conformance is not task legitimacy or natural-language equivalence. |
| [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/), Recommendation Apr. 2013 | Defines an OWL vocabulary for provenance entities, activities, and agents. | Candidate vocabulary for source and transformation traces. | Provenance can show lineage without proving accuracy, consent, or authority. |
| IETF [RFC 9942 — COSE Receipts](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9942.html) and [RFC 9943 — SCITT Architecture](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9943.html), Standards Track, June 2026 | Standardize signed proofs about verifiable-data-structure state plus registration, transparency, and audit architecture for signed statements. | A stable substrate for append-only, independently checkable provenance and correction records attached to SPEAR artifacts or consequential actions. | A valid receipt proves only the specified inclusion, consistency, or registration property. It does not prove statement truth, legitimate authority, consent, task correctness, execution, or complete history without the required monitoring and proofs. |
| [W3C ODRL Information Model 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/), Recommendation Feb. 2018 | Represents permissions, prohibitions, duties, parties, assets, constraints, and conflict strategies. | A directly relevant comparison for machine-readable authority and constraint fields. | Policy expression is not enforcement; rights vocabularies do not by themselves encode human intent. |
| [W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/), Recommendation May 2025 | Defines interoperable, cryptographically verifiable claims and explicitly separates verifiability from truth. | Useful pattern for provenance and asserted authority without conflating signatures with legitimacy. | Not a delegation language; verification does not prove the claim, consent, or task appropriateness. |
| [OAuth 2.0 Rich Authorization Requests, RFC 9396](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396.html) (2023), [Resource Indicators, RFC 8707](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8707.html) (2020), and [OAuth Security BCP, RFC 9700](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9700.html) (2025) | Standardize structured authorization details, resource-bound access, and current OAuth security practices. | Concrete precedents for narrow action/resource/privilege scopes and least privilege. | Authorization plumbing does not establish goal correctness, instruction meaning, or institutional legitimacy. |
| [ISO/IEC 24707:2018 Common Logic](https://www.iso.org/standard/66249.html), confirmed 2024 | Specifies a family of first-order logic languages for logic-based information interchange. | A semantics-preserving interchange reference for a possible formal SPEAR layer. | High authoring burden; operational mappings and human usability remain separate problems. |
| [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), W3C Recommendation Dec. 2024 | Supplies testable web accessibility success criteria. | Public SPEAR tools and documentation should be evaluated against it. | Web conformance does not validate instruction semantics or cognitive accessibility in every context. |
| [OpenAPI Specification 3.2.0](https://spec.openapis.org/oas/v3.2.0.html), Sept. 2025 | Defines a language-agnostic interface description intended for humans and computers. | A mature precedent for paired human-readable and machine-actionable contracts. | Describes APIs rather than goals or authority; prose and schemas can still disagree. |
| [Model Context Protocol 2026-07-28](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28) and [changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog), current release | Specifies stateless agent-to-tool requests, per-request capabilities, discovery, schemas, and optional extensions; its safety section requires user control and treats tool annotations as untrusted. | A likely transport substrate for SPEAR constraints and audit metadata. | MCP explicitly cannot enforce all consent and safety principles at protocol level; interoperability is not legitimate authority. The overview also says extensions negotiate “during initialization” although this release removes initialization, an apparent documentation inconsistency. |
| [Agent2Agent Protocol 1.0](https://a2a-protocol.org/v1.0.0/specification/) and [v1.0.1 release](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/releases/tag/v1.0.1), v1.0.0 Mar. 2026, v1.0.1 fixes May 2026 | Specifies discovery, Agent Cards, tasks, artifacts, bindings, authentication, and in-task authorization for agent-to-agent communication. | A possible carrier for SPEAR contracts between agents, complementary to MCP’s tool boundary. | Project maturity claims are not independent evidence; identity and capability metadata do not prove legitimate authority or correct goals. |
| [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10), Jan. 2023; revision underway | Provides the voluntary Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage risk functions. | A governance wrapper for evaluation and deployment claims around SPEAR. | Organization-level voluntary framework, not a protocol or conformance test. |

## Empirical findings and evaluation methods

| Source and provenance | Source fact | SPEAR relevance (Researcher inference) | Important limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amershi et al., [“Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction”](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/), CHI 2019 | Synthesized 18 guidelines and evaluated them with 49 practitioners across 20 products. | Correction, feedback, control, and expectation-setting should surround any SPEAR interface. | Practitioner/design-guideline evidence, not a causal study of agentic task contracts. |
| Bansal et al., [“Does the Whole Exceed Its Parts?”](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/does-the-whole-exceed-its-parts-the-effect-of-ai-explanations-on-complementary-team-performance/), CHI 2021 | In controlled classification tasks, explanations increased acceptance of both correct and incorrect AI advice without improving complementary team performance. | Readability and explanation cannot substitute for outcome testing. | Narrow tasks and interfaces; does not directly evaluate specification languages. |
| Buçinca et al., [“To Trust or to Think”](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml), *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction* 5(CSCW1), 2021, N=199 | Cognitive-forcing interventions reduced overreliance but were often less preferred. | CHECK or STOP gates may help while imposing measurable usability and accessibility costs. | One task and study population; effect may not generalize to long-horizon agents. |
| Zamfirescu-Pereira et al., [“Why Johnny Can’t Prompt”](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bjoern/papers/zamfirescu-johnny-chi2023.pdf), CHI 2023 | Qualitatively studied ten non-expert users building chatbots and documented recurring prompt-design difficulties. | Direct evidence that authoring abstractions and feedback matter for non-experts. | Small, system-specific qualitative sample; not a protocol-effect estimate. |
| Mavin et al., [EARS](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/easy-approach-to-requirements-syntax-ears/), RE 2009 | Proposes five constrained-natural-language requirement templates and reports industrial use in an aero-engine control project. | The closest established “pidgin” precedent for reducing requirement ambiguity while retaining readable prose. | Limited case evidence; no explicit authority or contemporary LLM semantics. |
| Zhou et al., [IFEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911) and [primary code/data](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/instruction_following_eval), 2023 | Introduces roughly 500 prompts spanning 25 objectively checkable instruction types with deterministic graders. | A model for mechanical acceptance tests and separate constraint-level outcomes. | Mostly surface, lexical, and format constraints; not latent intent, authority, or open-world safety. |
| Jiang et al., [FollowBench](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.257/), ACL 2024 | Evaluates following of incrementally added, fine-grained constraints across several task families. | Supports testing interaction effects as specifications gain constraints. | Constructed static-output tasks; constraints are not necessarily independent or authority-sensitive. |
| Qin et al., [InFoBench](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.772/), Findings of ACL 2024 | Decomposes 500 instructions into 2,250 requirements for fine-grained evaluation. | Useful precedent for requirement-level diagnostics rather than a single aggregate score. | Some evaluation depends on human or model judgment; decomposition quality is itself an assumption. |
| Debenedetti et al., [AgentDojo](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/97091a5177d8dc64b1da8bf3e1f6fb54-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html), NeurIPS 2024 | Provides 97 realistic agent tasks and 629 prompt-injection security cases in a dynamic tool sandbox. | A concrete benchmark pattern for utility and security outcomes under untrusted tool content. | Simulated applications, selected threats, and version-sensitive agents do not establish field safety. |
| Jia et al., [Task Shield](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1435/), ACL 2025 | Evaluates a task-alignment prompt-injection defense on AgentDojo; the paper reports low attack success while retaining task utility. | Shows that tool actions can be checked against a task representation. | Model-based alignment judgment is not proof of authority; results are benchmark and model dependent. |
| Purpura et al., [MOSAIC](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.62/), EACL 2026 | Evaluates prompts with up to 20 heterogeneous constraints and reports constraint-type, count, position, and model interactions, including different order effects. | Strong reason to randomize condition order and report per-constraint rather than only aggregate adherence. | Synthetic tasks; mixed mechanical and LLM-based scoring; limited model/application sample. |
| Wang, Poskitt, and Sun, [AgentSpec](https://cposkitt.github.io/files/publications/agentspec_llm_enforcement_icse26.pdf), ICSE 2026 | Introduces a trigger/predicate/enforcement DSL for agent runtime rules; generated rules achieved high precision but materially lower recall in the reported embodied evaluation. | A direct comparison for executable SPEAR safety constraints and fail-closed enforcement. | Generated-rule omissions matter; evaluated domains and simulations are bounded. |
| D’Amour et al., [“Underspecification Presents Challenges for Credibility in Modern Machine Learning”](https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/20-1335.html), JMLR 2022 | Shows that pipelines can admit many predictors with similar held-out performance but different deployment behavior. | Motivates stress tests and multiple equivalent renderings rather than one aggregate benchmark. | Focuses on predictive ML pipelines, not human-authored command languages. |

## Conceptual foundations

| Source and provenance | Source fact | SPEAR relevance (Researcher inference) | Important limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shannon, [“A Mathematical Theory of Communication”](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x), *Bell System Technical Journal*, 1948 | Formalizes information, entropy, channels, and coding independent of message meaning. | Grounds claims about compactness and transmission cost. | Explicitly does not solve semantics, intent, authority, or human effort. |
| Tishby, Pereira, and Bialek, [“The Information Bottleneck Method”](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057), 1999/2000 | Frames compressed representations that preserve information relevant to a target variable. | Formal inspiration for short contracts that preserve task-relevant distinctions. | Relevance variables must be chosen correctly; human authoring and governance costs are absent. |
| Grice, [“Logic and Conversation”](https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789004368811/BP000003.xml), 1975 | Develops cooperative conversational maxims and implicature. | Explains why pragmatic cues and shared assumptions can defeat surface-equivalence checks. | Assumes cooperative norms and common ground that tool-using agents or adversarial inputs may lack. |
| Clark and Brennan, [“Grounding in Communication”](https://doi.org/10.1037/10096-006), 1991 | Describes how interlocutors establish sufficient mutual understanding under different communication constraints. | Motivates acknowledgement, repair, and clarification mechanisms. | Human collaborative communication; not a model of asymmetric authority or nonhuman agents. |
| Lewis, [*Convention*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9780470693711), 1969/2002 edition | Analyzes conventions as coordination equilibria. | Frames SPEAR tokens as conventions that require stable shared interpretation. | Abstract account; no implementation, usability, or enforcement evidence. |
| Zave and Jackson, [“Four Dark Corners of Requirements Engineering”](https://doi.org/10.1145/237432.237434), TOSEM 1997 | Distinguishes environment assumptions, requirements, and specifications. | Supports separating human goals, world facts, and machine contracts. | Classical engineered systems; assumes boundaries that can be murkier in agentic settings. |
| Jackson, [Alloy language and analysis](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/sdg/pubs/2002/alloy-journal.pdf), TOSEM 2002 | Uses relational logic and bounded analysis to find counterexamples. | Candidate backend for constraint consistency and small-scope counterexample searches. | Bounded checking is not proof beyond the modeled scope; formalization can omit real concerns. |
| Lamport, [*Specifying Systems*](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book.html), 2002, with official [TLA+ overview](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/spec-and-verifying.pdf) | Presents temporal specification and model-checking methods for concurrent systems. | Candidate backend for long-horizon state, safety, and stop conditions. | Substantial authoring burden; results depend on the model and explored configuration. |
| Saltzer and Schroeder, [“The Protection of Information in Computer Systems”](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/protection/), Proceedings of the IEEE 1975 | Articulates least privilege, fail-safe defaults, complete mediation, and psychological acceptability. | Foundational design tests for AUTHORITY, STOP, and tool permissions. | Access-control principles do not resolve semantic intent or legitimate institutional authority. |
| Nissenbaum, [“Privacy as Contextual Integrity”](https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/wlr/vol79/iss1/10/), 2004 | Frames privacy around context-appropriate information flows and transmission principles. | Prevents reducing privacy to secrecy alone when SPEAR carries context across tools. | Contextual norms require substantive social and legal judgment; they are not automatically derivable. |
| Hadfield-Menell et al., [Cooperative Inverse Reinforcement Learning](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/c3395dd46c34fa7fd8d729d8cf88b7a8-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2016 | Models a robot uncertain about a human reward in a cooperative game. | Formal precedent for treating expressed instructions as incomplete evidence about objectives. | Idealized shared-payoff assumptions; not an authority, consent, or protocol model. |
| Hadfield-Menell et al., [Inverse Reward Design](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/32fdab6559cdfa4f167f8c31b9199643-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2017 | Treats a designed reward as evidence about an intended objective rather than the objective itself. | Supports the boundary that SPEAR cannot recover unexpressed values merely from a written contract. | Toy environments and strong inference assumptions; limited operational guidance. |
| Birgisson et al., [Macaroons](https://research.google/pubs/macaroons-cookies-with-contextual-caveats-for-decentralized-authorization-in-the-cloud/), NDSS 2014 | Introduces decentralized bearer credentials with attenuating contextual caveats. | Concrete mechanism for narrowing delegated capability without expanding it. | Cryptographic authorization token, not natural-language meaning, consent, or task correctness. |
| Pang et al., [Zanzibar](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc19/presentation/pang), USENIX ATC 2019 | Describes Google’s globally distributed relationship-based authorization system. | Shows how authority relationships can be checked independently of an LLM. | One large-scale implementation; relationship membership does not encode consent or task semantics. |

evidence.

Textual intent is not enforced authority; function parameter constraints/defaults are not necessarily enforced; transient derived state can affect outcomes without a durable record; remote imports add provenance, availability, and denial-of-service risks; the Rules security appendix remains incomplete. |

| Peng, [“Programmable Prompting Structure”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25379), arXiv Mar. 2026 | Directly compares structured intent templates across tasks, languages, and models. | Unreviewed; conditions change information content and temperature, judge family overlaps generation, and reported units may not be independent. Not validation of SPEAR. |
| Ashfaq et al., [Intent-Governed Tool Authorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22916), arXiv June 2026 | Proposes user intent as a narrowing layer over static policy, with intent certificates and draft-first writes. | Preprint tied to one project; needs independent implementation, threat-model, and evaluation evidence. |
| [W3C RDF 1.2 Concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/) and [Semantics](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-semantics/), Candidate Recommendation snapshot Apr. 2026 | Emerging semantic-interoperability substrate. | Candidate, not final; authoring cost, tooling, and adoption remain uncertain. |
| Zhan et al., [“When Memory Becomes Authority” / AuthMem-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01679), arXiv v2 Aug. 2026 | Uses paired histories that hold a claim and downstream task fixed while varying source authority; the authors report authority collapse in 48 of 49 model/consolidator configurations. It directly tests SPEAR's premise that provenance and use-specific authority must survive compression and reuse. | Unreviewed synthetic benchmark with simplified authority classes, model-dependent results, and one controlled memory-to-action cycle; no standalone public benchmark repository was verified in this scan. |
| Mercado and Lomuscio, [“Formal Verification of Agentic Systems over Operational Data”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03609) ([reproduction repository](https://github.com/alejandro-mercado/stead-reproducibility)), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Formalizes Stateful Tool-Enabled Agentic Deployments and derives finite-domain preservation and PSPACE-complete verification under boundedness, tool uniformity, and identifier-renaming equivariance. It connects SPEAR state, approval, and stop fields to temporal properties over complete workflows. | Unreviewed; the guarantees apply only to the modeled single-agent relational system and stated assumptions. Canonicalization is graph-isomorphism-hard, and the case study is not deployment validation. |
| Wang et al., [WeClawArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03499) ([public artifact snapshot](https://anonymous.4open.science/r/WeClawArena-541D)), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Introduces 124 cross-user tasks expanded to 620 matched variants and reports task utility separately from privacy, poisoned-evidence, and invalid-authority-path outcomes. Its matched multi-principal design is a candidate pattern for testing SPEAR delegation without collapsing utility and harm. | Unreviewed simulated workspaces; attack outcomes include LLM judgment with limited author-only human checking, some payloads are restricted, and the anonymous artifact URL may not be durable. |
| Feng, Zhao, and Crisan, [IntentLint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04331), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026; manuscript identifies itself as UIST 2026 | Represents inferred analytic intent as structured, editable shared rules and checks later prompts for conflicts; a formative study and 16-participant controlled study provide early HCI evidence for intent scaffolding at action time. | Short individual sessions over pre-populated notebooks, flat roles, no longitudinal test of stale or conflicting rules, and false-positive linting. The study evaluates collaboration experience rather than analytic correctness; proceedings status was not independently verified. |
| Chen et al., [“Allow” to Achieve, Over-Privileged Inadvertently](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04755), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Evaluates task-conditioned mobile-agent permission choices in 1,072 Android-style dialog trials; controlled requester and task-context changes materially altered grants, and prompt mitigations were inconsistent. | Unreviewed; one agent framework, five applications, synthetic popups, limited tasks and permissions, and expert labels rather than diverse user preferences. It does not evaluate the proposed separate authorization layer. |
| Wang et al., [SCP-NL2TL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05439) ([project page](https://sites.google.com/ucr.edu/scpnl2tl); [public review package](https://github.com/libbywang9/SCP_NL2TL), created 10 Aug. 2026), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Adds an accept-or-abstain layer to natural-language-to-temporal-logic translation using back-translation, repeated semantic checks, conformal risk control, and an input anomaly screen across STL, LTL, and SpaTiaL. The author-linked package adds scored STL data and an offline reproduction entrypoint. | Unreviewed. Its guarantee is marginal joint risk in expectation under exchangeability, not conditional error among accepted outputs; individual resplits can exceed the target and full abstention is possible. The package covers only the STL subset and has no explicit license, so auditability improved without establishing reuse permission or full-paper reproducibility. |
| Chen et al., [“When Experience Becomes Instruction”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05563), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Treats promotion of trajectories into persistent skills as a trust boundary and reports that repeated workflow-aligned inert-canary evidence can enter artifacts in two self-evolving skill systems. | Unreviewed and limited to artifact creation, not runtime compromise; two trajectory-based systems, one evolution cycle, inert canaries, and no verified standalone reproduction repository. It does not cover user-scoped, verifier-only, or parameter-training systems. |
| Rauchfleisch and Jungherr, [“Toward Meaningful Transparency for AI Chatbots”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11794), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026; [preregistration](https://osf.io/wge5h/overview?view_only=f96453f50e5248c5804a7586e013e014) and [data/code](https://osf.io/k985j/overview?view_only=e572307709a3465c8c7a7e1ea6ba2bf6) | In a preregistered randomized experiment with 1,500 UK adults, an AI-identity label was practically equivalent to no disclosure, while a disclosure bundle covering persuasive purpose, method, and concealment instruction reduced the reported immediate attitude shift from 13.1 to 6.3 points. | Preprint; one short interaction, one model/prompt, selected persuadable policy issues, and a UK sample. The treatment bundles three disclosure components, so the study does not identify which component caused the effect or establish durable agency protection. |
| Li et al., [WebRider / RiderBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06704) ([CC BY 4.0 artifact](https://huggingface.co/datasets/WebRider/WebRider)), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Represents live-web delegation as contracts over goals, constraints, evidence obligations, answer form, stop rules, and task-local persona controls. Across 4,096 contracts on 42 sites, the authors report 99.2% task finalization but 38.8% all-contract success, with the artifact retaining contracts, screenshots, guarded actions, state, and labels. | Unreviewed; policies are supplied rather than elicited, live sites and access failures drift, some gates use model judgments calibrated on 300 two-expert rollouts, the human comparison used six convenience-sample raters, and results depend on controller and date. |
| Wu et al., [SpecPath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09799), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026 | Holds repository, final contract, executable verifier, agent configuration, and budget fixed while varying contract-equivalent requirement histories; 35 of 100 complete blocks that passed a direct specification failed at least one equivalent history. | Unreviewed five-family Python study with synthetic histories; only 127 of 210 possible core-history blocks were complete and executable probes do not prove full program equivalence. The manuscript says artifacts are released but gives no verifiable public location. |
| Bu, [Security Principal and Verifier Binding for Agent Communication Protocols, revision 06](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-bu-agentproto-security-principal-binding-06), Individual Internet-Draft posted 17 Aug. 2026 | Separates human/organizational authority, live instance identity, delegated scope, tool identity, session state, and action evidence into carrier, verifier, binding, freshness, accepted-result, failure, and evidence fields. Revision 06 adds composite-strength, dependency-closure, and row-outcome guidance. | One-author, expiring work in progress intended as informational guidance; not an RFC, adopted working-group consensus, wire format, implementation, or validated authorization mechanism. |
| Xabier Muruaga, [“Bounded Agents: Delegation Security for Multi-Agent AI Systems”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15888), arXiv v1 Aug. 2026; [Apache-2.0 implementation](https://github.com/xmuruaga/bounded-agents) | Proposes an external Agentic Principal Chain that binds pre-declared intent, attenuates delegated scope and budgets, and checks accumulated prior-action state. ## Maintenance rule

At each weekly cycle, record additions, removals, and changed status with reasons. Prefer a
smaller canon whose claims can be audited over a longer citation list. A source moves from
provisional only when its provenance, method, and relevance justify the move; citation count
alone is never sufficient.
